# E — Philosophy: hands teach, vision protects

All three thinkers are **paraphrased and attributed**; no verbatim quotations. Sources to cite: Aristotle, *Parts of Animals* IV.10 (where he reports and answers Anaxagoras) and *On the Soul* III.8; Giordano Bruno, *Spaccio de la bestia trionfante* (1584).

**Anaxagoras** held that humans became the wisest of animals because they have hands: the hand came first, intelligence followed.

**Aristotle** answered the other way round: nature gave us hands because we are intelligent, since only an intelligent being can use the most versatile tool. He called the hand the instrument that uses all other instruments.

**Giordano Bruno**, in the *Spaccio*, praised the hand as what lets human beings act on the world and remake it, the organ through which the mind becomes work.

## The link to our project
For 2,500 years the argument was whether the hand makes the mind or the mind makes the hand. A humanoid robot settles nothing, but it shows the two halves apart:
- **The hand without the mind** is a Dex3 hand driven by a glove: fast, faithful, blind to what it holds.
- **The mind without the hand** is a vision model: it knows an egg when it sees one, but cannot touch it.

Our system puts them back together. **The human hand teaches** (Rokoko gloves carry motion, the Anaxagoras half). **Vision protects** (models decide how hard the hand may close, the Aristotle half). And, as Bruno would want, the result is work: repetitive work and delicate work, done by a robot that a non-expert can teach.

**Line for the pitch:** "Hands made us human. Now we lend ours to robots, and give them eyes so they don't break what they hold."
